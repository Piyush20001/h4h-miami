# Hurricane guidance for Miami-Dade residents (English source text)

## 1. Evacuation orders and recommendations
- Miami-Dade gives two kinds of instructions. An evacuation recommendation is for people who may need extra time, such as people with mobility challenges or people in flood-prone areas outside storm surge zones. An evacuation order is for specific areas at risk, based on the hurricane's track and expected storm surge.
- If local officials order your area to evacuate, leave immediately.
- Residents are encouraged to stay with family or friends inland, in a non-evacuation area, first. Evacuation centers are a "shelter of last resort."
- Do not walk, swim, or drive through floodwater. Six inches of fast-moving water can knock you down. One foot can sweep away a vehicle.
Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/service.page?Mduid_service=ser1477583540306215 and https://www.ready.gov/hurricanes

## 2. Finding an open evacuation center
- Centers open only for certain storms. Not every center opens every time.
- After an evacuation is announced, check miamidade.gov, call 311, or use the SAFE app to see which centers are open.
Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/service.page?Mduid_service=ser1477583540306215

## 3. What to bring to an evacuation center
- Each person gets about 20 square feet, so bring essentials only.
- Bedding or a cot, infant and child items, cash, hygiene items, medication, water, snacks, clothing, and any special dietary, elderly, or disability items.
- Eat before leaving. Bring fully charged devices, because outlets are limited.
Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/service.page?Mduid_service=ser1477583540306215

## 4. Basic supplies kit (ready.gov)
- Water: one gallon per person per day, for several days. Food: at least a several-day supply of non-perishable food.
- Battery-powered or hand-crank radio, flashlight, first aid kit, extra batteries, whistle, manual can opener, local maps, phone with chargers and a backup battery.
- Prescription medications, and eyeglasses.
- For infants: formula, bottles, diapers, wipes.
- Keep medication and pet supplies in your go bag or car. You may not have access to them for days or weeks.
Source: https://www.ready.gov/kit and https://www.ready.gov/hurricanes

## 5. Pets
- Pet-friendly evacuation centers are for residents of an evacuation zone, an unsafe structure, or a trailer. Pre-registration is not required. Centers are announced as needed.
- Bring: proof of residency (photo ID and a recent utility bill with your address), supplies for yourself and your pet, medical and current vaccination records, and proof of annual rabies vaccination. Dogs also need a visible Miami-Dade County dog license.
- Keep pets crated, leashed, or otherwise controlled. Muzzle aggressive or anxious pets.
- A veterinarian checks each pet on arrival. A pet judged a safety risk cannot enter.
- Up to four pets per household. At least one family member must stay in the center with the pet.
- Service animals are allowed at all shelters.
Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/service.page?Mduid_service=ser1598287646006415

## 6. No car: transportation
- Emergency bus pickup sites are marked with signs. Wheelchair-accessible buses run only between pickup sites and evacuation centers.
- If no site is nearby, take Miami-Dade Transit and tell the operator you are going to an evacuation pickup site. Public transit may stop three hours before the storm or when winds exceed 39 mph.
- Call 311 or use the SAFE app for center, bus, and assistance locations.
Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/service.page?Mduid_service=ser1477583540306215

## 7. Medical needs: Emergency and Evacuation Assistance Program (EEAP)
- For people who cannot evacuate on their own: those who need specialized transportation, are bedbound, depend on life-sustaining equipment that needs electricity, or need help with daily living, and have no one to drive them.
- It provides specialized transportation, safe shelter, medical monitoring, and wellness checks. A caregiver or companion must stay with the evacuee.
- Residents of nursing homes, assisted living facilities, and group homes are not eligible.
- Apply online, by mail, or call 311 to request an application (English, Spanish, Haitian Creole; Braille or large print available). Each eligible person submits a separate application. Registration closes before an evacuation order is issued, so apply early. Pre-registered residents get priority.
- EEAP registrant hotline: 305-513-7700. TTY/TDD: 305-468-5402.
Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/fire/eeap.asp
